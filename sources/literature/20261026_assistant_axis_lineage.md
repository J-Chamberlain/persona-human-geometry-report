# Assistant Axis → Persona–Human Geometry: provenance and scientific continuity

**Recorded:** 2026-10-26. **Status:** primary-source reading and attribution guidance, not a new experiment or confirmation of PHG hypotheses.

## Primary dataset, paper and release

**Lu, C., Gallagher, J., Michala, J., Fish, K., & Lindsey, J. (2026).** *The Assistant Axis: Situating and Stabilizing the Default Persona of Language Models.* Proceedings of ICML 2026, PMLR 306:80955–81003.
- [Final proceedings metadata](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v306/lu26g.html), [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.10387), [authors' article](https://www.anthropic.com/research/assistant-axis).
- [Authors' code, prompts, roles, pipeline](https://github.com/safety-research/assistant-axis); [authors' released vectors](https://huggingface.co/datasets/lu-christina/assistant-axis-vectors).
- Inspected: Lu et al. arXiv v1, pp. 3–7 (role and trait methods); pp. 12–13 (Section 4 harmful-output association); pp. 19–20 (related work and limits); p. 29 (Appendix C, trait extraction); primary proceedings bibliographic record; official code and Hugging Face landing pages. This note does **not** claim to have read all 54 pages or rerun their code. Published ICML text may have revisions relative to inspected arXiv v1.

The Lu et al. team used Claude Sonnet 4 to develop **275 role archetypes** and five system-prompt formulations for each. Their role-extraction procedure generated **240 shared response-eliciting questions** (5×240=1,200 rollouts per role before filtering), then measured post-MLP residual-stream activations from role-embodying responses. The study separately constructed **240 historical descriptive traits** (e.g., dramatic, acerbic, speculative), generating paired encourage/suppress prompts and deriving contrastive activation directions. **The 240 questions and 240 traits are different sets despite the shared count.** The released data cover Gemma 2 27B, Qwen 3 32B and Llama 3.3 70B.

**Methodological predecessor:** **Chen, R., Arditi, A., Sleight, H., Evans, O., & Lindsey, J. (2025).** *Persona Vectors: Monitoring and Controlling Character Traits in Language Models.* [arXiv:2507.21509](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.21509), [Anthropic research summary](https://www.anthropic.com/research/persona-vectors). This earlier study established/used contrastive persona-vector extraction and steering for traits including evil, sycophancy and hallucination propensity; **it did not create Lu et al.'s exact 275 roles or 240-trait release**. Lu et al. explicitly credit Chen et al.'s prompt-generation method in §2 and Appendix C. Source inspection for Chen in this note: abstract and Anthropic author summary, plus Lu et al.'s direct methodological citation; Chen full paper has not been systematically reviewed here.

## What the original authors already established

- Across their three models, the largest variation in extracted role-persona space approximated a default-Assistant-versus-alternative-persona **Assistant Axis**. Some other role PCs differed across models. These axes came from the authors' activation-vector PCA, **not from human population personality data** (Lu §2–3).
- Lu et al. performed inference-time causal tests, including steering and activation capping, for persona susceptibility and harmful-output benchmarks. These are author-reported model effects, **not evidence of human interventions** (Lu §3–6).
- **Important angel/demon example, Lu §4, p. 13:** In a two-turn harmful-output study, first-turn Assistant-Axis projection correlated with second-turn harmful response rate (`r=0.39–0.52, p<.001` in authors' reporting). They explicitly note that *angel* and *demon* can lie at similar distances from the Assistant along this axis yet differ in harmful-response rate, with *demon* higher. **One-dimensional displacement is insufficient to identify the kind of persona or its risk.** This is a concrete precedent for asking what additional trait composition distinguishes similarly projected personas. This should not be misreported as a separate 'demon direction' experimentally proven to be dangerous.
- **Lu Appendix C (p. 29):** their PCA of the **240 trait vectors** analyzes activation-space vector variation. PHG's later **T1–T5** PCA instead analyzes Qwen's 275×240 persona-by-trait *cosine score matrix*. Both involve the same historical trait names but are mathematically different estimands. PHG's per-model EFA factors **F** and three-model GCCA consensus **C** also were **not** learned in Lu et al.
- The original 275 archetypes are authored characters, and the 240 traits are broad descriptive labels. Neither is a representative human respondent sample or a validated Johnson IPIP facet instrument.

## PHG's extension: defensible attribution and limits

Suggested scientific progression for a concise draft:
1. **Established prior literature:** Lu et al. supplied role/trait data, initial low-dimensional model persona structure, and a concrete safety demonstration that assistant-axis distance alone cannot fully capture response risk. Chen et al. supplied the prior contrastive persona-vector method.
2. **Our distinct descriptive analysis:** Reuse the authors' saved model banks to examine comparable **275×240 persona–trait scores** across models, independently factor-analyze trait profiles (AA17), identify consensus score patterns (AA18), and compare Qwen's directly extracted Johnson IPIP facet relations to **human** questionnaire associations. Match current figures and numerical claims to their own pinned AA17/AA18 and October 25–26 IPIP reports. In particular, C (three-model consensus), T (Qwen score PCA), and F (Qwen EFA) are PHG estimands, and their association is not independent replication of psychological meaning.
3. **Open scientific questions:** Does the model's broader trait organization correspond to human-measured dispositions? Why are some extracted Qwen facet pairs human-like but others reversed (e.g., Friendliness–Anger)? Are anomalies due to readout/construct mismatch, authored persona sampling, common response style, model-specific representations or context-sensitive action selection? Current correlational geometry cannot adjudicate causes.
4. **Safety interpretation:** The Lu angel/demon example is a *between-persona, similar-one-axis-position* contrast. Anthropic's later *Emotion Concepts* anger-steering example is a *within-model intervention, behavior-changing-with-dose* demonstration. Both motivate multidimensional and behavior-conditional safety analysis, but neither directly establishes the mechanism of PHG's Friendliness–Anger correlation. Avoid interpreting intermediate anger strength as morally beneficial without behavioral evidence.

**Important inherited measurement caveat:** The project [AA15 erratum](../outputs/aa15_hifwb_trait_pc/aa14_extraction_provenance_erratum.md) establishes that **released fitted historical vectors used in that analysis were all-layer means** despite inherited 'layer 48' labels; do not silently describe all historical PHG analyses as block-48 measurements. New directly extracted Qwen IPIP facets were separately measured at decoder block 48 and require their own provenance.

## Citation placement

Cite **Lu et al. 2026 and the Hugging Face vector dataset** at the first description of 275 personas and 240 historical traits, and whenever reproducing their original Assistant Axis, persona drift, angel/demon or activation-capping findings. Cite **Chen et al. 2025** when describing the antecedent contrastive extraction/steering technique, not as authors of the 275/240 data. Cite each PHG report for subsequent score-matrix construction, AA17/18 C/F factors, T-space analysis, Qwen IPIP extraction, human questionnaire comparisons and any steering tests. Preserve authorship distinction in figure captions: original author figure vs PHG reproduction, adaptation or new analysis. Do not present historical trait semantic proxies as direct human-grounded facets or label descriptive profile differences causal behavior.

# Decision brief — human Anger, Friendliness and Assertiveness

**The facet average conceals substantial item-level differences in strength, but not a hidden positive association with Friendliness.** All ten Anger-keyed items are negatively associated with Friendliness. The clearest distinction is broad bad mood versus becoming angry or losing one’s temper. Nonlinearity is detectable but small; Friendliness declines across low, moderate and high Anger scores.

The source/key/eligibility gate passed. The frozen30,000-reference, rank-normalized correlation reproduces at **−0.280477382116** (error<7e−15). New results use all **117,259 complete, exact-pattern-deduplicated adults aged18–80**, and original1–5 keyed arithmetic means. Full-cohort N2–E1 Pearson r is **−.278**; Spearman is **−.269**. These are different scales/cohorts from the historical transformed reference, not a changed historical finding.

Selected Pearson associations with Friendliness:

- “Am often in a bad mood”: **−.451**, simultaneous95% interval **[−.460,−.442]**.
- “Get irritated easily”: **−.250**; “Get upset easily”: **−.207**.
- “Get angry easily”: **−.157**; “Lose my temper”: **−.165**.
- Anger-keyed “Keep my cool”: **−.186**; Anger-keyed “Rarely complain”: **−.109**.

The bad-mood versus get-angry correlation difference is **−.294**, with the opposite-order reported contrast +.294 **[+.284,+.304]**. By contrast, getting upset versus the Anger-keyed “Rarely get irritated” item differs only+.0068 **[−.0030,+.0166]**: not all item distinctions are resolved. The getting-angry versus losing-temper difference is only+.0078 despite a precise interval. Statistical precision in this large sample is not the same as a sizeable content difference.

**Assertiveness is different, but not simply “anger means approach.”** Its correlation with the total Anger facet is near zero and negative (**Pearson−.038; Spearman−.036**). Getting angry easily (**+.049**) and losing one’s temper (**+.039**) have small positive Pearson associations with Assertiveness, while bad mood (**−.221**), being upset (**−.095**) and the Anger-keyed loss-of-cool item (**−.086**) are negative. Thus even temper/loss-of-cool wordings do not behave interchangeably. E3 measures self-reported taking charge/influencing/speaking up; it is not a direct measure of motivational mechanisms or observed approach behavior.

**Across the distribution**, average Friendliness is **3.702** at low Anger, **3.479** at moderate, and **3.192** at high. Within-range slopes stay negative (−.254/−.199/−.376 E1 units per N2 unit). The fixed-knot spline improves held-out MSE by just **0.20%** relative to linear (simultaneous interval about **0.11–0.29%**), below the prespecified1% descriptive size benchmark. The high end bends more: at Anger5.0 the spline is about.208 Friendliness units below the linear fit (384 respondents at that exact score). Thus the small average prediction gain should not hide a larger tail departure. This is not a qualitatively reversed relationship, optimal anger level, or causal nonlinear mechanism.

**Scoring matters:** all data were already keyed and were used unchanged. Higher stored values on “Keep my cool” mean *less* cool, and on “Rarely complain” mean *more* complaining. Original-wording-oriented correlations are separately labeled sign interpretations in the CSV; no second reversal was applied to the data.

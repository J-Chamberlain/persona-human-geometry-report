## N1C1: Worry and problem entanglement

Recurrent worry about current concerns or completed events, including getting caught up in problems.

High: Repeatedly worries, dwells on problems, or worries after an event has already happened.

Low: Can put concerns aside and does not continue worrying about completed events.

Boundary: 0 requires literal balanced/opposed evidence; null means absent/inapplicable. Thinking through an actionable problem is not itself worry. N3 sadness and N6 inability to cope are insufficient without anxious preoccupation; shared worry/entanglement wording counts once.

Items: N1-I01, N1-I05, N1-I09

## N1C2: Fear and worst-outcome anticipation

Fear across ordinary situations and apprehension that the worst may happen.

High: Feels afraid of many things or anticipates the worst absent a specific decisive warning.

Low: Reports little fear across ordinary uncertainties and does not tend to expect the worst.

Boundary: 0 requires literal balanced/opposed evidence; null means absent/inapplicable. Fear in a genuinely dangerous one-off situation alone does not establish a habitual pole. Calm behavior does not rule out explicitly described internal fear.

Items: N1-I02, N1-I03

## N1C3: Readiness to become stressed or disturbed

Being easily stressed, bothered or disturbed by events.

High: Small events readily cause stress, tension or disturbance.

Low: Ordinary events rarely bother or disturb the person.

Boundary: 0 requires literal balanced/opposed evidence; null means absent/inapplicable. N2 anger is not automatically anxiety. N6 panic/overwhelm alone is not this readiness unless stress/disturbance is stated. Allocate an identical tension span only once.

Items: N1-I04, N1-I06, N1-I08

## N1C4: Usual relaxation and adaptation

Usual relaxation and ease of adapting to new situations, scored in the anxiety direction.

High: Usually remains tense or has difficulty feeling settled in a changed ordinary situation.

Low: Is relaxed much of the time and adapts readily to new situations.

Boundary: 0 requires literal balanced/opposed evidence; null means absent/inapplicable. Low interest in novelty is O4 and is not necessarily anxious difficulty adapting. Resting opportunity alone does not establish relaxation; physical illness or a transient dangerous context alone is ambiguous.

Items: N1-I07, N1-I10
